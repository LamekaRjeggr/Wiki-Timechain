# Closed dossier: the “U.S.–Israel military merger” story

This lab treats the supplied narrative as its complete evidence universe. Participants
must not add outside facts. The exercise tests event modeling, not the narrative's
real-world accuracy.

## Stage 1 — sparse initial report

- On May 13, 2026, the House introduced the FY2027 NDAA as H.R. 8800.
- It contained a U.S.–Israel defense-technology initiative.
- On May 29, critics began discussing the provision, then numbered Section 224.
- Early descriptions alleged that the proposal would “merge” the U.S. and Israeli
  militaries.

## Stage 2 — parallel text and procedural history

- The House provision was later renumbered Section 219.
- Its described scope included joint research, testing, production, procurement
  pathways, and integration of some jointly developed or Israeli-origin technologies
  into U.S. systems.
- By June 4, a similar Senate initiative was known as Section 1217.
- The supplied narrative says neither proposal created joint command, transferred U.S.
  troops to Israeli command, or transferred American military authority to Israel.
- Thomas Massie and Ro Khanna introduced an amendment to remove House Section 219.
- House leadership did not allow that amendment a floor vote; Congress did not take a
  separate yes/no vote on Section 219.

## Stage 3 — package vote and current status

- On July 21, the House approved the rule governing debate on H.R. 8800.
- On July 22, the House passed the complete FY2027 NDAA, 216–212, with Section 219 still
  included.
- On July 29, the supplied narrative characterizes the supported distinction as: no
  military merger, but a proposed expansion and formalization of joint defense research,
  production, and parts of the defense supply chain.
- As of September 1, 2026, the House version had passed and the Senate draft contained
  its similar Section 1217, but the Senate had not passed the final NDAA, there was no
  final House–Senate agreement, and the President had not signed it.
- The proposal was therefore not law at the dossier cutoff. Reconciliation, another
  vote in each chamber, and presidential signature or veto remained possible future
  steps.

## Modeling cautions

- A bill's introduction, public discovery of a provision, a procedural rule, a package
  vote, and enactment are different events.
- Renumbering Section 224 to Section 219 is not necessarily a change in substance.
- “Military merger” is a reported description, not a neutral event title.
- Passage of the whole House NDAA is not a separate vote on Section 219.
- The July 22 House vote is not enactment.
- Statements about September 1 status belong at the cutoff; predicted fall actions are
  not completed timeline events.
